LAB 12: QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

1. Why did adding a 25% serial section drop the two-core speed-up from 1.99x down to 1.59x?

Answer:

With a 25% serial part, the measured speedup was 1.59x (243,991 us in Run 1 / 244,019 us in Run 2 vs. 389,088 us / 389,087 us single-core), close to Amdahl's theoretical value of 1.60x (1 / (0.25 + 0.75/2)).

2. Why did the unlocked shared counter lose updates (1,190,150, 1,983,099, 1,993,160) instead of reaching 2,000,000, and why did the result change on every run?

Answer:

Because thread interleaving and timing across Core 0 and Core 1 are not deterministically synchronized without a lock, different runs lose different numbers of increments, producing different totals (1,190,150, 1,983,099, and 1,993,160 instead of 2,000,000).

3. Why did the locked counter produce exactly 2,000,000 every time, yet take approximately 10.4x longer (1,315,962 us vs. 126,208 us)?

Answer:

The critical section utilizes a hardware spinlock (portENTER_CRITICAL / portEXIT_CRITICAL) that enforces mutual exclusion across both CPU cores. Only one core can execute the read-modify-write sequence at any given microsecond, while the competing core spins waiting for the lock.

This completely eliminates race conditions, guaranteeing an exact final count of 2,000,000 across all test runs.

However, acquiring and releasing hardware spinlocks for 2,000,000 individual operations introduces severe bus and lock-contention overhead. Serializing access negates the performance benefits of multi-core execution, resulting in an execution latency of 1,315,962 us (10.4x slower than unsynchronized execution).


4. Why did the float sum differ slightly between one core (666967552.00) and two cores (666854656.00)?

Answer:

Floating-point arithmetic is non-associative: (A + B) + C != A + (B + C) due to intermediate finite-precision rounding at each step.

In single-core execution, N = 1,000,000 terms are accumulated in sequence into a single accumulator.

In dual-core execution, Core 0 and Core 1 independently accumulate partial sums of 500,000 terms across separate ranges, which are subsequently added together. Because intermediate magnitudes grow differently in the two halves, the point at which lower-order bits are shifted off and rounded differs between single-core and dual-core partitioning.

This small discrepancy is standard floating-point behavior rather than a hardware error.
