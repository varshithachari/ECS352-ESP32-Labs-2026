1. In this lab, I understood how an 8-bit ALU performs arithmetic and logical operations such as ADD, SUB, AND, OR, XOR, NOT and SHL, while generating status flags like Zero, Carry, Sign and Overflow.

2. I learned that these flags help the processor identify important conditions in the result, especially the difference between unsigned carry and signed overflow.

3. The shift-and-add multiplication method was tested against normal multiplication for all 65,536 possible 8-bit input pairs and produced zero errors, proving that multiplication can be implemented using simpler operations.

4. The CPU cycle measurements showed that integer operations required approximately 11.0 to 17.1 cycles, while floating-point division required 58.2 cycles in this experiment.

5. Integer / (16.1 cycles) and % (17.1 cycles in the verification table) took more cycles than + and *, and float / (58.2 cycles) took the most cycles among the tested operations.

6. This helped me understand that the ESP32 can perform several integer and floating-point operations efficiently, but the execution cost depends on the type of operation and the processor's implementation.

7. One interesting observation was that dividing negative numbers by 8 and shifting them right by 3 produced different results, such as -17 giving -2 through division and -3 through right shifting, because division truncates towards zero while arithmetic right shifting preserves the sign bit.

8. What surprised me most was that a simple-looking right shift could produce a different result from division, showing why understanding low-level processor operations is important when writing reliable code.
