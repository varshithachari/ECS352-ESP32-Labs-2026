1. In this experiment, I explored parallel computing on the ESP32 by running tasks across both Core 0 and Core 1.
2. When the square-root summation workload was distributed across both cores, execution time dropped from 389,088 us to 195,665 us, demonstrating an effective dual-core speedup of approximately 1.99x.
3. Introducing a 25% serial portion limited the overall speedup to 1.59x, which closely matched the theoretical prediction of 1.60x given by Amdahl's Law.
4. Without a lock, race conditions on the shared counter caused lost updates, giving 1,190,150, 1,983,099, and 1,993,160 across runs instead of 2,000,000.
5. Enabling a critical-section spinlock ensured atomic increments and consistently yielded exactly 2,000,000 counts across all runs.
6. However, mutual exclusion introduced substantial overhead, increasing execution time from 126,208 us to 1,315,962 us (a ~10.4x penalty).
7. I also noted that floating-point sums differed slightly between single-core (666,967,552.00) and two-core (666,854,656.00) runs due to the non-associative nature of IEEE 754 addition.
8. This lab clearly illustrated that achieving parallelism requires managing synchronization costs and Amdahl bottlenecks.
