1. Through this experiment, I learned how 32-bit integers are stored and represented in binary form inside a computer.
2. I understood that signed 32-bit integers can store both positive and negative values, ranging from -2,147,483,648 to 2,147,483,647.
3. I observed how negative numbers such as -5 and -1 are represented using the two's complement method.
4. I saw how adding 1 to INT32_MAX produces the bit pattern 0x80000000 (INT32_MIN), showing why signed overflow is dangerous in C/C++.
5. I also learned that an unsigned 32-bit integer can store values up to 4,294,967,295, and adding 1 to this maximum value produces 0.
6. I understood that signed and unsigned data types can interpret the same binary pattern differently.
7. By converting int8_t -1 into int32_t, I observed sign extension, where the sign bit is extended to the larger data type.
8. Similarly, converting uint8_t 255 into int32_t demonstrated zero extension, where the extra bits are filled with zeros.
9. The conversion of int8_t -1 into uint32_t showed how a negative value can become 4,294,967,295 when interpreted as an unsigned integer.
10. Overall, this experiment helped me understand binary representation, integer limits, overflow and type conversion through practical execution on the ESP32.
