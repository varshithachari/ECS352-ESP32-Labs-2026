1. This experiment helped me understand how binary addition is performed internally using logic gates instead of directly depending on the processor's addition operator.

2. I learned that a half adder uses XOR to calculate the sum and AND to calculate the carry, while a full adder handles two input bits along with an incoming carry.

3. By connecting full adders, we created an 8-bit ripple-carry adder that could perform addition and subtraction using the same basic circuit.

4. I understood that subtraction follows the two's complement method, where the second operand is inverted and an initial carry of 1 is added.

5. The exhaustive testing covered 65,536 input pairs and reported zero errors, which showed that our implementation correctly handled the tested addition and subtraction cases.

6. I also learned why Carry and Overflow must be checked separately, as unsigned arithmetic and signed arithmetic have different value ranges.

7. Simulating addition through gate functions took 257.5 cycles per addition, whereas the + benchmark took 14.3 cycles.

8. This experiment gave me a better understanding of arithmetic logic circuits, binary calculations and the importance of hardware optimisation in computer architecture.
