1. This experiment helped me understand the difference between temporary and permanent data storage in the ESP32.
2. The RAM counter started from 1 every time because its value was reset when the board restarted.
3. The Flash counter retained its previous value and increased to 2, 3 and 4 across the three runs.
4. I observed that RAM operations were very fast, with write times of 0–1 microseconds and read times of 1–2 microseconds.
5. Flash operations took longer, with writes around 703–704 microseconds and reads around 131–132 microseconds.
6. I understood that Flash memory is useful for saving important values that must remain available after a power cycle.
7. Avoiding frequent Flash writes keeps execution responsive and protects Flash against finite erase/write cycle wear.
8. I was surprised by how much faster RAM access was compared with Flash access.
