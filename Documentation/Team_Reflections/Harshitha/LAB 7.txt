1. In this lab, I learned how an 8-bit ALU performs arithmetic and logical operations such as addition, subtraction, AND, OR, XOR, NOT and left shift.

2. I understood how the four status flags Zero, Carry, Sign and Overflow indicate different conditions of the ALU result.

3. The shift-and-add multiplication method was tested for all 65,536 possible input pairs and produced zero errors compared with normal multiplication.

4. From the CPU cycle measurements, I observed that integer operations took around 11.0 to 17.1 cycles, whereas floating-point division took 58.2 cycles.

5. This showed me that different operations have different execution costs and that division generally requires more processing than simple addition or shifting.

6. Different integer and floating-point operations showed noticeably different cycle counts depending on the operation tested.

7. An interesting result was that -17 divided by 8 produced -2, while shifting it right by 3 produced -3 because integer division truncates towards zero.

8. This experiment helped me understand how processor operations, status flags and execution time are connected to the performance of a computer.
